# Museum scans cover bronzes, not the palace

**Licensed Sketchfab references cover the bronze props only, not the Qin palace itself.** The useful material is a set of CC0 museum scans of Warring States, Zhou and early Han bronzes, mostly from the Minneapolis Institute of Art (artsmia), plus one Cleveland ding, two Smithsonian Freer|Sackler pieces and a CC BY Princeton figure lamp. No CC0 or CC BY model of period-correct Qin architecture was found on Sketchfab or any other free source: no rammed-earth terrace, straight-eave grey-tile hall, 瓦当 eave, 铺首 door, 回廊 corridor, 阙 tower or trestle bridge. The same goes for the bronze chariot, lamellar armour, cranes, vats, 华表, water clock, throne or twelve giants. Every Chinese building with a CC BY licence and a known style is Ming–Qing or later. **Owner decision (24 Nov 22:00): every model, scan, photo and texture listed here is reference only, never copied 1:1 or shipped; all game assets are our own designs.** The data supports that choice on technical grounds as well. The usable scans run 15k–577k triangles, against prop budgets of 500–3,000 triangles in the project's asset list. The GDD decides what exists. The useful takeaway from each source is therefore proportion, silhouette, ornament layout and material colour, and the final table maps each source to the asset-list item (A–C) it informs. Licences are still noted, as a reference-use note. CC0 sources carry no strings. A CC BY source needs a credit only if a design follows it closely. NoAI-marked pages must not be fed into AI tools. One limit applies to everything below: **the licence-filtered Sketchfab API search was never run** (a proxy 403 from the shell, and approval for page fetches was not available). The list is therefore partial, and every "not verified" licence must be opened in a browser before relying on it.

**Warring States pieces fit best.** The Qin court used Warring States-style bronzes: Lei, Fanghu, Bianhu, Winged Dragon, Tiger, Mythological Beast and Shengding. Shang and Zhou vessels read as plausible ancient heirlooms. The Han xianglu and Princeton lamp are about one generation after Qin. The triangle counts show why reference-only is also the right technical call. The asset list sets C1 guardian beasts at 3k/1k/300 tris, C2 ding at 2k/600, C3–C6 at 1.5k/500 and C9 lamps at 500, which is tighter than the 5–10k single-prop target in the brief. The scans would need 7.5x (Cleveland ding) to over 1,000x (Princeton lamp) reduction. Their photoreal textures would also clash with stylised geometry. The value is in what a modeller reads off them: how deep a Warring States ding bowl sits on its legs, where the taotie bands run, and what colour the green-blue patina takes over bronze. C1 asks for its own design anyway.

Three more models carry usable licences but are weak references. [Chinese Bronze Ding Vessel](https://sketchfab.com/models/300371f38b92494fbb2a5f1709db2104) by 3DandVR (CC BY 4.0, 1.1M tris) is a scan of a modern Beijing souvenir copy, so its proportions are not authentic. [Chinese Bell Optimized](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/chinese-bell-optimized-d5e1a97c065645d286690d52f6493e03) by topfrank2013 (CC BY 4.0, 32.3k tris, optimised from a scan by Geoffrey Marchal) is of unknown period, and its bell type is unconfirmed (probably not a bianzhong, a guess). [Ding censer with an openwork cover](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/ding-censer-with-an-openwork-cover-1034cd8e4df5430cb874f012415a43b0) by the National Museum Kraków (CC0, 133k tris) is Qianlong-era Qing and the wrong period.

## Figures, armour and vehicles: no licensed Qin source at all

**No CC0 or CC BY Sketchfab model of a terracotta warrior, Qin lamellar armour, a Qin helmet, a ge, ji, jian or crossbow, or the 铜车马 bronze chariot was confirmed.** The best-looking warrior scans are Corey Jaskolski's Digital Preservation Project scans: the [General](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/general-ded2697cd0a2413a95fa5a9b478bc99c), the [Kneeling Archer](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/kneeling-archer-9746027fbe934fab9576930a037257c3) and the [Cavalryman](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/cavalryman-6bad328d1b7f4c63bf6f85f2bf8f7e9f), about 59k triangles each. They show no licence and no download button. They remain the best view-only references for lamellar plate rows, armour hem lines and the kneeling-crossbowman pose. One confirmed CC BY warrior exists off Sketchfab: [Warrior 2 on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10240599) (CC BY 4.0, one 355.8 kB GLB). It is a 123D Catch scan of a souvenir soldier, so it is a weak reference, and its triangle count was not stated. For the twelve bronze giants, the best period fit is artsmia's [Standing figure, 5th–4th c BCE](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/standing-figure-5th-4th-c-bce-3df2c64714644242a080893b209289e2) (64k tris, downloadable). It is a good silhouette and robe-proportion reference, but it is **CC BY-SA**. A design that follows it closely could count as an adaptation bound by share-alike, so take only general proportions from it.

Two CC0 rigs give useful benchmarks for skeleton layout, clip list and triangle density. Quaternius' [LowPoly Animated Knight](https://quaternius.itch.io/lowpoly-animated-knight) is CC0, in FBX, OBJ and Blend, with idle, walk, run, attack, death and more; its triangle count is not stated. System G6's [Modular RPG Characters](https://opengameart.org/content/modular-rpg-characters) are CC0, **2–3k triangles with 512–1024 textures**, and come with about 45 clips and an IK rig. They were made in Blender 2.79b, and one commenter only saw the idle clips in 2.83. Under the owner rule these show what a 10–15k-budget soldier needs, without being shipped. Our own soldiers and giants would carry lamellar plates, helmets and weapons as bone-parented static meshes.

## Coverage gap: the filtered sweep never ran

The search was not systematic. All three Sketchfab passes hit the same block. `api.sketchfab.com/v3/search` returned a proxy 403 from the shell, and the WebFetch route needed user approval that was not available. The scans-and-textures pass also had WebFetch blocked, so no museum policy page was opened. **Every result came from web-search hits followed by single page fetches.** The `license=cc0|by&downloadable=true` sweep could find more artsmia, Smithsonian or Cleveland bronzes. ## Conclusion

The gap is not about licences. The open-access 3D record of early China consists of portable museum objects, mostly ritual vessels, so nothing exists for architecture, furniture or the Qin court's signature monuments. Under the owner's reference-only rule, that changes what a fuller API sweep can deliver. More CC0 bronzes would sharpen A13 and C1–C6, but no sweep will supply references for B1, B9 or B10. Those need other period sources: published excavation drawings and Han tomb reliefs and models.

The reference-only rule also narrows which licences matter. Close tracing of the CC BY-SA standing figure and the no-licence or NoAI pages is the real risk. Credit lines are needed only where a design follows a CC BY model closely (the Princeton lamp is the likeliest case). CC0 and verified public-domain sources should therefore be the primary references wherever two sources cover the same feature.
