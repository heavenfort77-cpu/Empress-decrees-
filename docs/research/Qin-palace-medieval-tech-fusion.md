# Ground Kaymuse's machines in excavated Qin craft

The excavated Qin palace was not a black castle. It was a set of **timber halls wrapped around stepped rammed-earth terraces**, with **red-painted floors, white-grounded painted walls, grey unglazed tile roofs with round cloud-pattern tile ends, and dragon-pattern hollow-brick steps**. Black belongs to the court: Qin took the water phase, so **clothes, banners and flags were black** ([The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_3199936)). For Empress Decrees, that gives the strongest look and the most defensible one: Kaymuse's black-clad court and black banners stand against yellow earth, cinnabar floors and grey roofs, and the burning reddens and chars those surfaces, which is exactly what archaeologists found at Xianyang ([The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1250319)). The "medieval fortress" layer can be built from Warring States sources rather than borrowed from Europe. Rammed-earth walls, wide moats, Mohist counter-tunnel listening jars, traction trebuchets and multiple-bolt crossbow carriages all date to the 4th century BCE or earlier, and the traction trebuchet that Europe calls medieval reached the Mediterranean from China. The fantasy machines read as native when their look comes from attested Qin metalwork (the 3,500-part bronze chariots, batch-cast crossbow triggers, gold and silver inlay) and their "waking" comes from attested legends (Yan Shi's automaton, the Lintao giant omen). Several palace pieces the game wants (stone lions, 华表, 门海 vats, bronze cranes, the water clock, the tiger tally) were not dated or researched in the notes. They should be re-made in Qin materials and labelled as invention, not presented as Qin history. Everything below fits low-poly meshes with klad-painted albedo textures at a jam budget.

Throughout, **Sourced** marks a finding backed by a cited source in the notes, and **Inference** marks a design conclusion built on those findings. Uncited claims from the notes are listed only under research gaps.

## Excavation shows earthen terraces and red floors; legend supplies the black and the fire

### What the ground actually holds (sourced)

The Xianyang palace city measured about **870 m east-west by 500 m north-south**, with more than 20 rammed-earth building bases inside, and its buildings stood mainly on rammed-earth high platforms (gaotai) ([Wikipedia: Xianyang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianyang_Palace)). Palace No. 1 includes a platform about **60 x 45 m** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianyang_Palace)). The excavation lead describes it as a **three-storey terraced building**: winding corridors at ground level, smaller rooms with wall tiles and washbasins on the second level, and a main hall of more than 60 m² on top ([China Daily, 2021](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202102/24/WS60359431a31024ad0baaa951_2.html)). The excavation report's figure list confirms rooms, cellars or pits, north and west corridors, a drainage pool, **underground pottery water pipes and floor drains**, a mural fragment, carbonised silk, and iron and bronze objects ([Science Press report sample](https://www.ecsponline.com/yz/B99EEE6B7579A4D02A12B6F1EB8E5DA2D000.pdf)). Scholars interpret No. 1 as a twin-tower gateway across a gully, No. 2 as the empress's palace and No. 3 as an administrative core, but these labels are interpretation, not inscriptions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianyang_Palace)).

Surface finish is unusually well recorded. The report names floor-tile patterns per palace: **米-grid, lozenge-grid, square grid, bi-disc with curled cloud, sun and cord patterns** ([report sample](https://www.ecsponline.com/yz/B99EEE6B7579A4D02A12B6F1EB8E5DA2D000.pdf)). **Hollow bricks served as stair treads and corridor barrier bricks**, decorated with single and paired dragons around bi discs, fish-dragons, a tiger-deer-frog scene and geometric patterns ([report sample](https://www.ecsponline.com/yz/B99EEE6B7579A4D02A12B6F1EB8E5DA2D000.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianyang_Palace)). Eave tile ends carry **cloud, animal, deformed-sunflower and plain** designs ([report sample](https://www.ecsponline.com/yz/B99EEE6B7579A4D02A12B6F1EB8E5DA2D000.pdf)), and a large Qin tile end at the Met measures **37 x 51.5 cm and weighs 13 kg** ([Met 640822](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/640822)). Murals were painted in three layers (mud plaster, white ground, paint) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianyang_Palace)). Palace No. 3's corridor murals ran over **30 m across nine bays**, one piece 32.4 m long, showing chariots and horses, ceremonial guards and a wheat-ear scene ([CNA, 2016](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/acn/201612290084.aspx)); the report adds attendant women, **"strange animals"**, a corner-tower mural and border designs ([report sample](https://www.ecsponline.com/yz/B99EEE6B7579A4D02A12B6F1EB8E5DA2D000.pdf)).

Building No. 6, a candidate main government hall, gives the best scale numbers: its top stands about **11.3 m above the Qin ground surface** on a near-square base of about 16,000 m², with L-shaped walls with pilasters, steps and ramps, **red-painted floors (涂朱地面)** and stone pillar bases ([The Paper 10834551](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_10834551); [The Paper 10833937](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_10833937)). The wider palace zone was bounded by a 4.5 km artificial ditch and a terrace edge over 20 m high, and a **9 km east-west water system, partly identified as Lanchi (兰池)**, ran through it ([The Paper 10833937](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_10833937)); a wooden bridge crossed the Wei ([China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202102/24/WS60359431a31024ad0baaa951_2.html)). Metalwork appears too: hollow cast-bronze sleeves (釭) clamped timber posts and beams into earth walls at a 7th-century BCE Qin palace site in Fengxiang ([Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/228595/architectural-fitting-gong)), and a bronze door-knocker plate (铺首) is shown at the Xianyang site museum ([zh.Wikipedia 咸阳宫](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/咸阳宫)). The Shiji adds what the ground cannot show: **flying corridors (飞阁), covered passages (甬道) and elevated causeways** let the emperor move unseen, and later texts mapped the palaces onto the sky, with the Wei bridge as the Milky Way ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianyang_Palace)).

### The fire is real at Xianyang and literary at Afang (sourced)

Charred wood and reddened walls were found at the Xianyang palace sites in the 1970s ([The Paper 1250319](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1250319)). Afang (阿房宫) is the opposite case. The 2002-2007 team found only a **giant, unfinished front-hall platform about 1,270 x 426 m and about 12 m high**, with no Qin tiles on top and, in the excavator's words, no trace of burning ([The Paper 1250319](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1250319)). Another summary gives about 1,320 x 420 m and 8 m high ([Wikipedia: Epang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epang_Palace)); the difference reflects survey and edge definitions. Du Mu's 9th-century poem, with its palace "covering 300 li and blocking out the sun", is literature ([The Paper 1250319](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1250319)). One 2025 finding is a gift to this game: the Afang platform was **built on a drained pond or lake bed**, its silt treated and the foundation rammed in layers ([Shaanxi gov, 2026](https://en.shaanxi.gov.cn/news/sn/202601/t20260120_3606357.html); [China Daily, 2026](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202601/20/WS696ec870a310d6866eb349c2_2.html)).

### The tomb and the giants: text with partial physical support (sourced)

The Shiji describes the tomb with **mercury rivers made to flow by machine, crossbows that shoot intruders automatically, constellations above and the land below, and lamps of "man-fish" fat** ([Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-booby-traps-of-qin-shi-huangs-tomb-fact-fiction-or-something-even-better-44580)). Soil surveys support the mercury: about **250 ppb average over the chamber, up to 1,500 ppb, against a 30 ppb background** ([Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/features/flowing-rivers-of-mercury/8122.article)). The surveys disagree on which quadrants are strongest ([Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/features/flowing-rivers-of-mercury/8122.article); [Sina 2003](https://news.sina.cn/sa/2003-11-28/detail-ikkntiak8701747.d.html)). Remote sensing found an enclosing **rammed-earth wall 16-22 m thick and about 30 m high, rammed in 5-6 cm layers**, around a chamber about 15 m high ([Sina 2003](https://news.sina.cn/sa/2003-11-28/detail-ikkntiak8701747.d.html)). The crossbows, star ceiling and lamps stay unverified because the chamber is unopened ([Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-booby-traps-of-qin-shi-huangs-tomb-fact-fiction-or-something-even-better-44580)). The twelve giants (十二金人) are Shiji-attested: weapons from the conquered states were melted into twelve figures **"of the type of bell stands"**, about 1,000 dan (about 30 t) each. The **11.5 m height comes from a later source**, no images survive, and the posture is never given ([Wikipedia: Twelve Metal Colossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Metal_Colossi)).

### What this means for the build (inference)

Kaymuse's "court in black" is historically right when it means **people, banners and lacquer**, not walls. The palace itself should read as yellow-brown tamped earth with visible layer lines, red floors, white plaster with painted bands, and heavy grey tile. A major hall sits 6-12 m up on its terrace. The burning state can be built from the evidence itself: char the timber, redden the earth walls. The drained-lake foundation of Afang gives a native reason for the palace to sink "wing by wing into a black lake": the lake is reclaiming ground taken from it. The giants are free to design, as long as they carry bell-stand beams and visibly melted weapons.

## A Qin fortress layer needs no European castle, only Mohist doctrine

### Walls, moats and gates up to Qin/Han (sourced)

Up to Qin and Han, Chinese walls were **rammed earth tamped in moving wooden frames on rubble footings**, and baked brick or stone facing on those walls came only in the Song and later ([Wikipedia: Chinese city wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_city_wall)). The barbican (瓮城) with an archery tower is a Ming and Qing form ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_city_wall)). From the 6th century BCE, walls gained towers, monumental gates and crenellations; rammed-earth walls reached about **5 m high and 25 m thick**, and moats about **50 m wide and 6 m deep**, with a course of river stones against erosion ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1147/fortifications-in-ancient-chinese-warfare/)). The Wei Liaozi's doctrine was simple: deep, wide moats and solid, thick walls ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1147/fortifications-in-ancient-chinese-warfare/)).

### The Mozi defence chapters as a level list (sourced, with unverified device names)

The Mozi's city-defence chapters (52-71) are organised by threat: **gate defence, attack from an elevation, ladders, inundation, sallies, tunnelling, the "ant-rush" of massed climbers, flags and pennants, commands and orders** ([ctext Mozi index](https://ctext.org/mozi/ens)). Secondary sources confirm several devices: **leather-covered pottery listening jars buried in the walls** to hear enemy tunnelling, and **mustard smoke blown by bellows** down the attackers' tunnels ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1147/fortifications-in-ancient-chinese-warfare/)); bed crossbows used defensively by the Mohists ([Wikipedia: Chinese siege weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_siege_weapons)); and heavier crossbow artillery worked by pulleys and winches from the 4th century BCE ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1147/fortifications-in-ancient-chinese-warfare/)). The named devices 籍车, 转射机, 连弩之车, 藉幕 and 悬门 could not be checked against the primary text (see gaps).

### Period-correct engines and what is later (sourced)

The safe set for a Qin-era palace is **cloud ladders** (Lu Ban, 5th century BCE, hinged and counterweighted, some with a crossbow compartment) ([Wikipedia: Lu Ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_Ban); [Wikipedia: Chinese siege weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_siege_weapons)), **siege towers** (from the 6th century BCE), **traction trebuchets** described in the Mozi, with wheeled versions said to need 200 pullers ([Wikipedia: Chinese siege weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_siege_weapons)), a **multiple-bolt crossbow on a three-wheeled carriage** described around 320 BCE ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_siege_weapons)), and a **Chu two-shot repeating crossbow** from a 4th-century BCE tomb ([Wikipedia: Repeating crossbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repeating_crossbow)). Later than Han, and therefore out unless deliberately labelled: the Zhuge Liang crossbow attribution (a misattribution), counterweight trebuchets (1273), hook carts (Three Kingdoms), Greek fire (about 900) and gunpowder (10th century) ([Wikipedia: Repeating crossbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repeating_crossbow); [Wikipedia: Chinese siege weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_siege_weapons)). The traction trebuchet that reads as "medieval" to Western players was in the eastern Mediterranean by the late 6th century CE, when more than 50 were used at Thessaloniki ([Wikipedia: Mangonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangonel)). The Western "medieval" siege engine can therefore appear as Chinese technology without any anachronism.

Mapped onto the room types, the Mozi gives a native rhythm (inference): tunnels where the listening-jar sound is the 0.4 s tell, an earth ramp rising toward the wall answered by a bolt carriage, a wall-top wave against ladders, a flooding room, and sally ports. The 号令 and 旗帜 chapters suggest the palace alarm state should be shown as signal flags. The Mohist role, a defensive engineer for hire, fits the jam theme, because the building itself is the machine that is out to get the player.

### How other works got the blend wrong (sourced)

Critics of *The Great Wall* (2016) faulted a Song-era story with a Ming-looking wall, a wall that hydraulically lifts to expose spinning razor blades, and a plot implying China lacked lodestones it had studied since the Han; they praised the colour, banners and massed choreography ([Tor.com](https://tor.com/2017/06/07/medieval-matters-the-great-wall-white-saviors-and-lizard-dogs-from-space)). Games Workshop's Grand Cathay team said they deliberately avoided making it "the Empire in the East, with guns and cannons" ([Warhammer Community](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/wkn8afkh/old-world-almanack-the-iconography-of-an-eternal-empire/)). The lesson for this game (inference) is that fantasy machinery grown from real devices such as crossbow carriages and listening jars reads better than invented spectacle, and the wall should look like Qin earth, not like the Ming wall or the Forbidden City.

## Attested Qin metalwork gives the machines their look; legends give them their waking

### The attested base (sourced)

Qin's own workshops are the strongest anchor. Each of the two **near-half-size bronze chariots** from the mausoleum has **more than 3,500 parts**, made by casting, welding, drawing, inlaying and polishing, with gold and silver ornaments and copper wire about 0.5 mm thick ([Science and Technology Daily](https://stdaily.com/web/English/2023-05/18/content_1923186.html); [Wikipedia: Qin bronze chariots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_bronze_chariots)). **More than 200 bronze crossbow triggers** from the Terracotta Army were batch-cast and assembled in a **"cellular" workshop system**, with filing marks and inscriptions ([UCL, 2014](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/terracotta-army/news/2014/mar/crossbow-triggers-and-imperial-craft-organisation); [Antiquity, Li et al.](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/DB32EADD77493B84692756138D21D036/S0003598X00050262a.pdf/crossbows_and_imperial_craft_organisation_the_bronze_triggers_of_chinas_terracotta_army.pdf)). That study does not establish full interchangeability, so "perfectly interchangeable Qin parts" overstates it. An Eastern Zhou bronze at the Met kept part of its **original brassy, corrosion-free colour** and was whitish, almost tin-like, when new; its ornament was made from **master pattern stamps**, with gold, silver and copper inlay ([Met 45634](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45634)). Inlay (错金银) means pressing gold and silver wire into cast or cut grooves and polishing them flush ([HKU UMAG](https://ulearninglabs.umag.hku.hk/en/dictionaries/cuo-jinyin-gold-and-silver-inlaying/)). Han **jade burial suits** used 2,498 jade plates tied with gold wire, and a later text ranks the wire by status (gold, silver, copper, silk) ([Wikipedia: Jade burial suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade_burial_suit)). The terracotta figures were painted over a **dark-brown lacquer ground** in a full mineral palette: lead white, ochre, cinnabar and red lead, **Han purple**, azurite, malachite and bone black ([TUM](https://arc.ed.tum.de/en/rkk/archive-emmerling/china-project-bmbf/subprojects-1990-2006/toom-of-the-first-emperor/painting-techniques)).

### The legend layer (sourced as legend)

The automata are all legend or late text. Yan Shi's automaton in the Liezi (compiled about the 4th century CE) was built of leather, wood, glue and paint with artificial organs: **removing the heart silenced it, the liver blinded it, the kidneys stopped its legs** ([Colavito, Giles translation](https://jasoncolavito.com/ancient-chinese-automaton.html)). Lu Ban's wooden bird "stayed up for three days" ([Wikipedia: Lu Ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_Ban)). Mozi beat Gongshu Ban in **nine simulated siege games**, and Chu called off its war ([Wikipedia: Mozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozi)). The south-pointing chariot has no pre-Han or Han description; Ma Jun (3rd century CE) is its first reliable maker ([Wikipedia: South-pointing chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-pointing_chariot)). Ban Gu records that twelve giants in foreign dress appeared at Lintao in 221 BCE as an omen, and the statues were cast in their likeness ([Wikipedia: Twelve Metal Colossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Metal_Colossi)).

### How modern works make fantasy tech read as native (sourced)

Ken Liu describes silkpunk as a technical "language" of **bamboo, shells, coral, paper, silk, feathers and sinew**, with a grammar that favours **biomimetics**: airships regulate lift like fish swim bladders, and submarines move like whales ([Ken Liu](https://kenliu.name/books/what-is-silkpunk/)). Grand Cathay's team avoided **"Western reproductions of Chinese design"**, consulted Chinese-language books, used **octagons, a bagua-derived Wu Xing compass and "logographic sigils" instead of calligraphy**, chose Tang-like layered plate over mail, deliberately altered dragon anatomy, checked colour meanings (black = water) for Chinese audiences, and encoded crew and origin in unit icons ([Warhammer Community](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/wkn8afkh/old-world-almanack-the-iconography-of-an-eternal-empire/)). Ne Zha 2's Tianyuan ding draws its ornament from Sanxingdui bronze lei and a **"deconstructed taotie"**, reinterpreting Warring States bronzes rather than copying them ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1016988/drawing-from-bronze)). Genshin's Liyue is a useful counter-example: it recombines real karst landscapes and Huizhou-style towns with **mostly Qing-era clothing**, aiming for "moderate fantasy" ([Wikipedia: Liyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liyue_(Genshin_Impact))). It reads as "Chinese fantasy" to players, but from the wrong millennium for this game.

### Applying it to Kaymuse's machines (inference)

The split is clean: **build the look from attested craft and the waking from legend.** The bronze tiger can be cast at chariot grade (segmented, welded seams, gold-inlay lines, fine chain) and carry Yan Shi's organ logic as readable weak points, each tied to one function: break the throat core and the roar stops, the eye core and it hunts by sound, the haunch cores and it slows. Dormant, it wears green patina; awake, the patina cracks away to fresh brass-gold, which encodes the "fresh = alive, patina = dead" rule in one transition. The four-legged tread machines have no attested Qin ancestor; their honest roots are chariot running gear (wheels, axles, harness bronze) and cloud-ladder counterweights, and by Liu's biomimetic grammar they should move like oxen or tortoises, not tanks. Their bodies can borrow ding and lei silhouettes, as Ne Zha 2 did for a prop. Every plate gets a stamped workshop mark, grounded in the inscribed trigger parts. The nine school suits follow the jade-suit rule: **small hard plates tied with a binding whose material signals school or rank**, on the terracotta lamellar base (the lamellar itself was not researched). The Mohist-versus-Gongshu rivalry is a historical frame for school-against-school machine design: Mohists build defence, Gongshu builds siege engines. The twelve giants should share one cast pattern and skeleton, which is on-lore for batch-cast Qin bronze. The owner's rule that "same style means distinct characters" still applies, so each giant needs its own head, shoulder beam and melted-weapon motif, for example two per conquered state.

## Klad-painted albedo, one texel density and reserved colours keep it buildable

### What the craft sources say (sourced)

The one practical hand-painting source recommends **albedo-only textures with light, AO and form painted in**, worked as flat colours, then shadows, then gradients and detail, under one light direction. It also recommends consistent texel density, shared UV space for small props, a per-prop choice between modelled and painted detail (painting usually wins at low poly), and **the lowest resolution that holds the detail** ([80 Level](https://80.lv/articles/studying-hand-painted-texturing-workflow-for-stylized-art)). Supergiant's Jen Zee made an early **colour script "to catch any oddities or weakness"**, chose ink over painterly work partly because it was faster, and treated art ideas as disposable until the gameplay hardened ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/behind-the-art-of-hades-we-value-artistic-integrity-and-excellence-in-artistic-craft-at-supergiant-however-were-first-and-foremost-a-game-design-lead-team/)). **Glazed roof tiles date from the Tang**, were standardised in the Song and spread on top-tier Ming/Qing buildings like the Forbidden City ([Wikipedia: Chinese glazed roof tile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_glazed_roof_tile)).

### Turning that into a jam pipeline (inference)

Klad's stroke painting fits the albedo-only method directly: paint lighting into the texture and render with Lambert or toon materials under 1-2 real lights, which also satisfies the project's light budget. Qin ornament happens to suit trim sheets: the master-stamp bands on the Met bronze, the floor-tile grids and the hollow-brick dragon panels are all repeating strips. One 512-1024 trim atlas per material family (bronze, timber and lacquer, earth and tile, plaster and mural) covers most surfaces, and instanced tile ends, columns and bricks keep draw calls low. Readability rules here are standard practice, not sourced: silhouettes tested as flat black shapes at play zoom, floors kept mid-dark and low-saturation, characters lit at top and rim, and **one hazard hue that never appears in decoration**. Cinnabar cannot be the hazard colour because it is the decoration colour, and decorative fire must not pulse. Mercury should stay a desaturated silver so that a saturated cyan hazard hue does not blur into it; this needs an in-engine side-by-side check.

## Research gaps the notes record

The notes leave these items **unverified**, so none of them appears above as fact. **Palace detail:** the commonly repeated Palace No. 1 figures (main room about 13 x 12 m with a central 都柱 post, a vermilion 丹地 floor, a bath room, wall fireplaces, cool cellars, a 6 m platform) are not confirmed in the primary report, which was not retrieved online. There is no pigment analysis of the Xianyang murals, the roof form is entirely inferred (no Qin roof survives), the kui-dragon "king of tile ends" diameter is unsourced, and the Fengxiang 釭 hoard's count and sizes are unconfirmed. The Shiji 复道/阁道 passages were not quoted, and the lodestone gate (磁石门) is treated as legend. **Fortress:** the Mozi primary text was never read (ctext and wikisource blocked), so the device names **籍车, 转射机, 连弩之车, 藉幕, 悬门, 渠答, 爵穴 and 行城 are unverified**, as are pre-Han caltrops and pits, drawbridges, 阙 and parapet terminology, the earliest 瓮城, Cao Cao's 霹雳车 and the exact Shiji crossbow-trap line; Robin Yates's studies were not consulted, and no European castle source was retrieved. **Engineering:** the Zhang Heng seismoscope, Han Feizi's kite anecdote, 物勒工名 maker-inscription law, terracotta modular production, chariot mechanisms, **water clocks**, Qin lacquer, **Qin lamellar armour**, the wooden ox (木牛流马) and Needham were not researched. **Precedents:** **The Legend of Qin (秦时明月) Mohist and Gongshu machine designs were not reviewed** (the fetch failed), nor were Kingdom beyond a review opening, Black Myth, Sekiro, Wo Long, Naraka, Hero, Total War: Three Kingdoms or Grand Cathay's war-machine notes. **Art practice:** there is no sourced value-contrast ratio, texel-density figure or colour-blind faction guidance. **Anachronism:** **stone lions, 华表, paper lanterns, 脊兽 roof beasts and Qin clothing were not dated**; the notes do not cover 门海 vats, bronze cranes or censers, or the tiger tally (虎符) object at all (only that Qin standardised tally sizes, per [Wikipedia: Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang)). **Licences:** the Met Open Access and Chinese museum image terms were not fetched. Smaller conflicts remain open: the Afang width (426 m against 407-420 m), the giants' weight (30 t against about 60 t) and the quadrants of the tomb's mercury hot spots.

## Conclusion

The research turns the game's central image around. The "black palace" is a costume, not a building. Historically, the black is worn by Kaymuse's court, and the palace it stands in is earth, cinnabar, plaster and tile. That contrast makes the court more readable and the burning more legible, because fire has warm, light surfaces to darken. The same move works for the machines. Players read them as native when every surface traces to a Qin workshop practice (casting seams, stamp bands, inlay, ranked bindings) and every impossible behaviour traces to a Chinese legend (organs that control functions, giants foretold by omen). The two layers never need to be mixed.

The other finding is that the anachronism risk sits almost entirely in the props. The architecture and siege layer can be built period-true from sourced evidence. Stone lions, 华表, 门海 vats, cranes, censers, the water clock and the tiger tally either are later or were never checked, so the safe rule is to cast them all in Qin bronze, lacquer and earth and treat them openly as Kaymuse's inventions. A short sourcing pass on those seven props, the Mozi device names and Legend of Qin is the cheapest way to close the remaining risk before art production starts.

**Machines, suits and giants**
- [S] All machines are segmented castings: visible mould seams and flanges, gold or silver inlay lines as panel lines, and stamp-band ornament repeated per segment from one trim sheet.
- [I] Stamp a workshop mark on every plate. Ornament uses deconstructed taotie, cloud scroll and invented sigils; no readable calligraphy.
- [I] Machines move by animal analogy: the tiger like a big cat; the four-legged tread machines like oxen or tortoises, with chariot running gear (wheels, axles, harness bronze) and a ding or lei-shaped hull.
- [I] Bronze tiger: patina when dormant, cracking to fresh brass-gold on waking; three "organ" weak points (Yan Shi logic), each tied to one function (roar, sight, legs) and each with a tell of at least 0.4 s; mark them with inlay, not neon glow.
- [S] Suits: small hard plates tied with a binding whose material marks rank or school (gold, silver, copper, silk cord). [I] Layered lamellar, no mail. [I*] The nine suits differ in outline first (crest, shoulder mass, weapon shape) and colour second; test as flat black shapes at play zoom.
- [S] Twelve giants: bell-stand bearers cast from melted weapons, about 11.5 m (later source), no surviving image. [I] One shared skeleton and body cast, with a distinct head, shoulder beam and weapon-fragment motif per giant; keep the low LOD for distance.

**Architecture by room type**
- **Halls [S]:** timber hall on a stepped rammed-earth terrace 6-12 m high (No. 6 is 11.3 m); red floor; white plaster walls with painted bands; pilasters; stone pillar bases; bronze sleeves (釭) at beam joints [I: imperial-era use less evidenced]; bronze door-knocker plates. [I] Hip or gable grey-tile roof, straight eaves, round cloud-pattern tile ends (instanced).
- **Courts [I]:** gates within gates: moat crossing, outer gate between paired 阙 [*], walled forecourt as kill zone with crenellated wall-walks, inner gate up on a platform. Earth walls on a river-stone footing [S]. Signal flags show the alarm state.
- **Corridors [S]:** 回廊 with hollow-brick dragon-pattern parapets and stair treads; lozenge, 米-grid or square-grid floor tiles; long mural bands of chariots, guards, attendants and "strange animals" over white ground; floor drains and pipe grates. Elevated covered walkways (复道/阁道) link wings [text-based]. [I] Listening jars set in the wall act as the audio tell for tunnel enemies.
- **Gardens [I]:** water terraces and ponds after the Lanchi water system [S]; the wheat-ear mural scene [S] can supply planting motifs. No garden source exists in the notes, so keep planting sparse and alpha-tested.
- **Bridge [S/I]:** a timber trestle bridge (a wooden bridge crossed the Wei) framed as the "Milky Way" crossing from later sky-mapping texts; no drawbridge.
- **Core [S as Shiji text]:** a buried chamber inside a rammed-earth wall rammed in 5-6 cm layers; mercury rivers in channels; star-map ceiling; man-fish-oil lamps as emissive cards; automatic crossbows on pressure plates with a visible tell [legend].
- **Burning [S]:** char the timber and redden the earth walls (as found at Xianyang); swap to the burnt state by material swap or vertex-colour blend, not new meshes. [I] Collapsing wings sink into the black lake, which reclaims a drained lake bed (Afang's foundation [S]).

**Props (all re-made in Qin materials and labelled as invention, since none is dated in the notes) [I]**
- Stone lions become segmented bronze guardian beasts with silver inlay. 华表 becomes a bronze-banded timber or earth pillar with cloud-scroll trim. The 鼎 follows the Ne Zha 2 method: real Warring States vessel proportions, deconstructed ornament. 门海 vats, cranes, censers and the water clock are cast bronze with stamp bands. The tiger tally is a split bronze tiger with inlaid lines, echoing the apex. Lamps are bronze with mercury reflectors, never paper.

**Avoid**
- Glazed yellow or green roofs, Forbidden City roof beasts [S: glaze is Tang and later].
- Brick-faced walls, Ming arched gate tunnels, the Ming-wall look [S].
- Gunpowder, cannons, Greek fire, counterweight trebuchets, the "Zhuge" crossbow name [S: later].
- Razor-blade or hydraulic walls (*The Great Wall*), and "Western reproductions of Chinese design" [S].
- Qing clothing and Liyue-style towns [S].
- Torii, pagodas, samurai lacing or katanas; sci-fi panel lines, neon, glowing circuitry [I].
- Green patina on live machines [I], cinnabar used as a hazard colour [I], and colour as the only cue separating schools [I].

**Budget and pipeline**
- Albedo-only klad painting: flat colour, then shadow, then gradient and detail, one light direction [S].
- One texel density game-wide with 2-3 fixed brush sizes [I*]; trim sheets for bands, tiles and bricks; 512-1024 atlases per material family [S/I].
- Lambert or toon materials, 1-2 real lights; instance tile ends, columns and bricks; keep the heaviest scene under 500 calls and 0.5M triangles.
- Make a colour script per area before building it [S]: halls black and cinnabar, mercury rooms black and silver, burning wings black, orange and smoke, lake black and teal.
